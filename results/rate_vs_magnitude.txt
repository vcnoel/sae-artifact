  VERDICT: the rate readout is substantially less sensitive to
  alignment than the magnitude readout. The protocol can recommend a
  readout, not just flag a confound.

  Caveat 1: flip rate is bounded and its variance collapses when the
  mean rate is near 0 or 1. Measured flip rates run 0.017-0.110, i.e.
  the rate readout is a RARE event here, so it has genuinely less power
  than KL and part of the smaller rho is that, not saturation. This is
  the main threat to the recommendation and is not resolved by the data
  in hand -- a design with flip rates near 0.5 would settle it.

  Caveat 2: the pre-registered ORDERING was KL > bounded-magnitude >
  flip. Read against |dtop| -- the actual bounded magnitude -- see the
  table above. The SIGNED dtop column is reported only to show why it
  must not be used: 46% of its values are negative, so a rank
  correlation against it measures direction rather than size and its
  per-latent median is ~0. An earlier version of this analysis used the
  signed column and read its near-zero correlation as evidence about
  saturation; it was evidence about signs.

  Caveat 3: this comparison is not fully like-for-like on normalisation.
  kpn is per unit perturbation norm; flip and |dtop| are not normalised,
  and a rate cannot be. Raw KL is shown alongside kpn so the size of that
  gap is visible rather than hidden -- note rho(pnorm, KL_raw)=0.70,
  i.e. raw KL partly tracks intervention size (Artifact 2).
